# Weekly Optimizer

## Intro
We are building the core "brain" of our autonomous fantasy hockey bot. This is the weekly optimizer module, the model which gives the agent the conviction, given a current "state" (my roster, state of current matchup, nhl schedule, set of free agents), to execute transactions. A transaction usually involves picking up one player and dropping another player with the goal of either upgrading the roster OR streaming (picking up a player with more projected points for this current week).

This is an optimization game on multiple levels. The first level is the most obvious; we want to win our given matchup, e.g. get more fantasy points than our opponent in the week of our matchup. However, there are also a couple other levels which are more minor but will be the difference between an okay model and one capable of beating veteran fantasy hockey managers.


## Factors to Balance
The first non-obvious factor to balance is the fact that we don't want to drop good players, or to take it further, players with the potential to be good. We aim to quantify this with the metric called "upside". This pretty much encompasses players who have good underlying numbers but lower minutes, in addition to young players with the potential to break out (there's often some correlation between these two), as well as players on hot streaks. Instances of players like this breaking out mid-season are rare, but often become the type of player you wish you held on to when you look back at the season retroactively. So this is one factor to balance; we should be slightly more reluctant to drop players that we have identified as having good upside.

Another non-obvious factor to balance is that we don't want to let other players get our good players. The high upside players is encompassed by this, but what I'm referring to here is perhaps some overall notion we might have to create of what a "replacement level" player looks like. Since we're trying to make this tool league-agnostic, we need to be able to factor in all types of league settings, so simply setting some fixed threshold for a replacement level player is not possible. The number of teams in the fantasy league can differ, as well as positional eligibility and roster slots. So a replacement level player needs to somehow be calculated. And afterwards, we need to make sure that the players we're dropping aren't much better than that replacement level player. Replacement level should include both opportunity and upside scores, and other than that players should be evaluated based on their projected fantasy points per game.

Beyond replacement level players, we also need to be able to modify the threshold at which we are willing to drop a good player for a bad player with more games. For example, take the super basic toy problem of player A averaging 2.5 fantasy points per game and having 2 remaining games this week, versus Player B who averages 4 fantasy points per game and has only 1 remaining game this week. From basic math, player A would produce more fantasy points and technically be more valuable to our team this week. But in the long run, we would much rather have player B on our team. In a scenario where we are super desperate to win this current week, that might be a trade-off that we would be willing to make. But in another matchup where we are already winning by a substantial margin, we would much rather hang on to the higher-producing player. As you can see, this would present some kind of optimization where we need to decide between what level of desperation we currently have, weighing that against what our current matchup state is, and how good the transaction would be from a pure fantasy value perspective for the rest of the season.

There are certain situations where we are very desperate to win for example, in the playoffs, we would always be desperate to win, and I have learned firsthand to never bank on any point lead. In addition, in high-stakes matchups, such as if you are in second place playing against third place, or some other situation similar, we might be more willing to sacrifice future matchups for this current matchup. We have already built out most of this feature in the desperation categorization, where we have four levels of desperation: The lowest one is where we completely scrap this week because either we are winning or losing by a large margin and can fully look onto next week. The other three desperation categories will change our willingness to make risky transactions, where the player that we are acquiring might be worse than the player we are dropping.

Most decisively and importantly we need to balance the three ideas of winning this current week, making our roster better overall, and preparing for the next week. I have already I alluded to each of individually, But this is probably the root or the highest level of abstraction in this weekly optimizer. Based on the current state, we will probably end up needing to modify certain variables which correspond to desperation and the threshold of a player that we are willing to pick up.

Then, we will likely be running some sort of loop which makes sure that we always have the current state loaded. And then from there, whenever a player goes above our conviction threshold (whatever that may be), we would want to pull the trigger and execute a transaction. And obviously, this is implying that we also have a potential candidate that we are willing to drop in order to pick up this player. We have a lot of this built out already, but it is mostly going down a path that I am starting to doubt a little bit. 

Another thing to keep in mind is that injuries can happen. In these cases, we will ideally want to move our injured player into an injury slot. In these cases, we would be able to pick up an additional player without dropping anyone. Usually, upon a player being injured, I would rush to make a transaction right away. I think in pretty much any case you will be presented with, this will be the best move, which is to either:
- Pick up the best player for the rest of the current week, if we still care about winning this week
- Pick up the best possible player for the next week
- Pick up the best possible player in general as someone that we would like to have on our team

Obviously, there are other fantasy managers too, with their own teams making transactions while we are playing this optimization game, and potentially limiting our possibilities. What I plan to do is to run this on some sort of cron job or some other periodic sync where it will pull transaction data from the yahoo API. We can walk through what this means from a technical standpoint later, as in what parts of our pipeline we will need to refresh. But for now, we're just concerned with the strategy aspect. Not all the players that we would want to pick up will be available by the time we would like to add them. Therefore, that makes this a little bit harder, and it makes for the requirement that this is some sort of flexible optimization, not just one calculation at the beginning of the week. The good part is that often other teams making transactions will offer a couple new players being added to the pool, which are the players that they decide to drop. Most of these will likely be players that we are uninterested in, but often a team will drop someone that they probably should have kept on their roster, making the mistake that I warned against when deciding what a replacement level player is and what players are too valuable to drop. In these cases, there is a three-day waiver time or something like that. It is configurable based on what league you are in, but you don't need to worry about this since the free agent availability refresh will handle this for you and include it in the state.

## Data Source
We have a few data sources that we can assume will be reliable for the purpose of focusing on one thing, which is the weekly optimizer. If we ever doubt the reliability of the data sources, you should bring it up, but not correct it, as that will be the job of a separate agent. The reliable data sources include a fantasy point per game projection of each player, as well as all their individual stats, including ice time, if that is ever required. Additionally, you would get a comprehensive status of the current matchup, which includes both my team's points, the opposing team's points, my team's players with remaining games, and the opposing team's players with remaining games, all down to the granularity of what day those games are being played on.

In addition, we would know how many transactions I have left, how many transactions the opponent has left, the NHL schedule, and any news of injuries or deployment changes. You would also get upside and opportunity scores, as well as the set of available free agents, with which we would be able to project fantasy point forecasts for each of their upcoming games.

## Firsthand Experience from Me
There is quite a bit of experience that I have gained over the years of playing fantasy hockey. One of which is that typically Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Saturdays have more games overall. Maybe we could take a look at the distribution of when games are played to get some idea of this, but it's not necessary. All you need to know is that those three days are the most common days that games will fall on. In a given week, if a team plays four games, that is considered usually the best possible schedule. There are rare occurrences where a team will play five games in a week, but that is super uncommon. When we stream players, there is the idea of "off nights," which is a day that doesn't have many games, and therefore, a team playing on that day will be more likely to be able to slot into your lineup than a player on a different team. Since Tuesday, Thursday, and Saturday are what we consider "on nights," the typical optimal schedule consists of a team playing Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Sunday. However, four games is usually valuable regardless of when they are playing. The typical transaction on a Monday would usually consist of picking up someone with at least two off nights and dropping someone with zero. However, it doesn't always work like this, as in different weeks, we are able to slot different players onto our lineups during the on nights. It is for that reason that we probably shouldn't really care about off nights, and I am more giving you this information just to acclimatize you to what a fantasy hockey schedule looks like. 

Also, a tiny bit more about the schedule: Teams will never play three nights in a row. Back-to-backs are uncommon but not too rare.

Let's say we are thinking about optimizing the amount of points we will get in a week, and let's just say, for the purpose of this discussion, that we have four players that we would be willing to drop and four possible transactions. In this specific scenario, it might make a lot of sense to make all four transactions on the Monday if we are able to optimize the amount of off-nights we're getting. However, not all weeks will look like this. There are other factors here.

For example, maybe some player gets injured, which leads to another player becoming a super valuable long-term add. In this case, we don't want to be left with no transactions to pick them up. In a similar vein, we might want to weigh goalie streams higher than player streams for individual nights, because goalies typically get higher variation in their scores, but more consistent production than a skater. If a given backup goalie ended up getting two starts, but neither of them were confirmed at the beginning of the week, we wouldn't have that data to go off of. And if we made all our transactions on the Monday, we wouldn't be able to capitalize on that opportunity. So, there is at least some value in waiting to make our transactions. In addition, we might not always have four slots, and we are open to dropping those players. Sometimes, we might have only one or two "droppable" players, and yet we still want to stream in some players for the current week. In these cases, our model might end up making two transactions on a Monday to optimize our schedule, and after that, realize that we won't be able to make any more transactions for now, because none of our players are droppable. 

## Leads
The structure for making transactions that I envision for this model is trying to mirror the process that I go about when making transactions in my leads.  I can imagine that at any point in time, we would want to first take a look at the state, which I have mentioned which variables correspond to the state previously. These will be the inputs to our module that we will assume to be correct.  based on this state, we will then determine a desperation level, as in, how badly do we want to win this week versus making our team better in general for the future.  once we have decided on that, it will set a threshold for how good the players we are willing to drop are. Based on this threshold, as well as the notion of a replacement level player at each position, and our current state and desperation level, we will then make a plan for the rest of the week, given the amount of ads we currently have. This will forecast what we are doing with each of our adds, or at least what we plan to do. 

Obviously, due to the dynamic nature of this game, we will not always go according to our plan. We should generate new plans based on the new states that we enter.  this should probably happen every single time  our plan needs to change because either we have decided someone is better than one of the players we are picking up as part of our plan, or one of them becomes unavailable, either due to injury or someone else picking them up. Changes to our plan can also include injuries to our own players, or even modifications to the NHL schedule. 

An example of a plan that I might make is something like:
- Drop player A for player B on Monday
- Drop player B for player C on Thursday
    
The context around this might be:
- Tues/Sat: full lineup
- Player A plays Tues/Thurs/Sat and is only slightly better than replacement-level
- Player B plays Mon/Wed/Sat, with Mon/Wed being free off-nights we can fit into the schedule
- Player C plays Tue/Thurs/Fri/Sun, so dropping B for C gives the extra 3 games that we wouldn't have otherwise

This is just an example, but you can see the type of foresight required to plan out a week. Maybe this would be using 1 of my droppable player slots, and I would also have another player I'd be willing to drop that has a mid schedule where I could get an extra game or two from a different stream. 

We could simplify this down to only looking at transactions on the team level. This is super important from the perspective of not wasting compute. Often I will look at the schedule and say "okay I want to pick up a player from X team on Y day, then drop them for a player on Z team on W day." If our plan first does some type of scan on the team level, and then compares possible plans against each other based on the quality of players that are available on those teams, that could be a huge avenue for a good algorithm. The other part of the plan is that it should likely have backup plans. For example I would want to pick up player X, but player Y also has the same 2 off nights I was looking for and they might be slightly worse but a viable backup plan. In the plan I would want to see what option 1, 2, 3, and maybe even 4 are.

One possibly non-trivial point is the notion that once the agent makes a plan, if will often probably make the first step towards executing that plan on the spot if it decides the plan is the best possible plan. It might even make the 2nd and/or 3rd (though I highly doubt I would ever make 3 transactions on a Monday, unless I have some players who are contributing virtually nothing to my team that week or something). The only scenario I could see me making 3 transactions on Monday is if my team was left in a horrible state from being desperate the last week and streaming like 2 backup goalies on the last day or something, who will all have 1 or no games this week.

Eventually we will have a screen where I can monitor the agent's plan and execution and state and stuff like that from the web app, so I would like this information to be organized cleanly for that possibility in the future.
